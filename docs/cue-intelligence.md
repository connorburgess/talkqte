# Cue intelligence and feedback

Status: evaluation plan with an optional [fictional Claude Code lab](claude-cue-lab.md), 8 November 2026. No reasoning model is connected to the app, and no production provider, conversational feedback system, or learning pipeline is selected. This supports the [participation goal](../PRODUCT.md#goal-and-first-proof) and [product readiness plan](product-readiness.md).

## What powers the prototype today

Rust's `suggest` function in `src-tauri/src/engine.rs` matches a few phrases in bundled fictional dialogue and returns fixed questions. It also raises an "Open question" cue quoting a sentence that ends with a question mark and opens with a group-addressing phrase ("Does anyone…", "Any thoughts…", "Who can…"), only when no fixed question matched the line, under the same expiry and pause as the fixed questions, and with its two-minute repeat rule keyed to the question text so an unrelated question is not hidden. A recognized owner, date, or choice later withdraws it, and a line that answers its own question raises nothing. Questions about the call itself ("Can anyone hear me?", "Who can see my screen?") are excluded by a short word list, so any question containing one of those words ("call", "screen", "audio", …) is skipped even when it is about the work. Questions put to one person by name, questions in other words, and transcripts that drop the question mark are missed, and in a live transcript the rule cannot tell the user's own question from someone else's. Rust also controls timing, hold, cooldown, and dismissal. A second fixed rule set withdraws an open question when a later sentence names an owner (“Jordan will take it”, where the owner is “I” or a capitalized name), a deadline (“by Friday”, “tomorrow”), or a choice (“let's go with the sign”). Sentences that end with or open like a question, or contain a negation or hedge such as “not” or “maybe”, never count; a missed answer only leaves the question up until it expires. A question the user is reading stays in place and is marked as answered, with the answering line as a source. The same question type is not shown again within two minutes of when it was last raised and shown, so a repeated trigger does not re-ask a question the user has just seen. A question withdrawn before it was ever shown does not count. A sentence that opens by changing the topic, such as "Moving on to the budget", "OK, next item", or "On another note", withdraws an unread question as stale. A question being read stays, and its repeat history is kept. Otherwise the repeat history starts afresh for the new topic, and the same line can raise a new question; if it raises none, the usual pause follows the withdrawal. The phrase must open the sentence, so "Before we move on…" or "The trucks are moving on Tuesday" do not count, and moving to the next slide or page is not a topic change. Questions, negations, and hedges do not count either. Since 13 November 2026 a sentence that opens by putting its subject off to a later occasion counts as a topic change as well: "Let's leave the cover choice for another meeting", "We'll park that for next time", "Let's come back to that later". It needs an opening ("let's", "we'll", "we can", "we will"), a putting-off verb (leave, park, save, table, defer, postpone, revisit, come back to), one to four words for what is put off, and the occasion closing the sentence, so "Let's leave the door open", "Let's table the proposal", and "Let's come back to the first draft" do not count. Neither does a sentence with a second clause ("Let's come back to the first draft and leave the budget for later"), one that keeps something open or sets time aside ("Let's leave the vote open until next week", "Let's save some time for later"), or one that opens with a name ("Alex, let's leave that for another meeting"). The rule does not check what is being put off: if the speaker puts off something other than the question on screen, an unread question is still withdrawn, and as with any topic change the repeat history starts afresh. "Let's park that until next week" is then quoted as a Said cue, because "until" with a time is a commitment; "for next week" is not quoted. A topic change described in other words is missed. An explicit correction that opens a statement as its own clause ("Sorry, I misspoke.", "Scratch that, …", "Correction: …", "I take that back", "Never mind —") withdraws an unread question only when that question came from the same speaker's line just before, within 10 seconds; the engine cannot tell what else a correction refers to, so it leaves any other question alone. Live transcripts label every line "Speaker", so there any speaker's correction right after the line counts. Words taken back earlier in the same line raise no question, including a question asked of the user, while what follows the correction still can. A question being read stays, and so does an "Asked you" question, which the user may still want to answer. The usual pause after a withdrawal applies, but unlike a topic change the repeat history is kept. A bare "Actually", a phrase that runs on ("Never mind the cost"), and a correction said without such a phrase are not recognized; the last is the `context-corrected` case below. When imported notes are present, a line that contains at least two distinctive words (five or more letters) from a note's title raises a "Context" reminder once per note per session: the companion shows the note's title and excerpt labeled with its date and "not from this conversation", lowest in priority and under the same expiry and pause as a question, so dismissing a reminder leaves the same pause before the next cue. Common meeting and function words never count, words with an apostrophe ("Robin's") never count, titles with many words need three matches, and words taken back or put to someone else by name do not count. This is a title-word match, not a judgment of relevance; it exists to test the context-reminder hypothesis in the [research notes](research.md#the-next-experiment) and the pilot comparison, which measure such reminders separately from clarification questions. The engine can also hold a session-only user name. A question with that name opening or closing the sentence ("Robin, can you take the draft?", "Hey Robin — could you…?", "What do you think, Robin?") becomes an "Asked you" cue showing the question. It counts as a question only if it ends with a question mark or opens with a question word after the name, so "Robin, the draft is done." stays quiet. It replaces an unread suggestion and ignores the cooldown, but not a question being read. It stays for 45 seconds instead of 18 so the user can answer. A topic phrase does not clear it, and only a newer direct question replaces it. Dismiss still works. The app shows no countdown, answer, or score. Questions naming someone else, or no one, do not become "Asked you". Once the user has set their name, a sentence that puts a question to someone else by name, such as "Alex, can you take the first draft?" or "Can someone take it, Alex?", raises no suggestion: it is theirs to answer and already says who is meant. Other sentences in the same line still can. A name here is one capitalized word set off by a comma; greetings, common openers ("Honestly,"), time words, months, words for a group that includes the user ("Everyone,"), and any word that could be the user (either part of "Sam Lee", or "Sammy") do not count. It matches on capital letters, so some matches will be wrong: "Budget, someone can take it?" is treated as addressed to someone. A question to the user by first name only, when Settings holds a full name, raises an ordinary suggestion rather than "Asked you". Two-word names, names a transcript writes in lower case, and names set off by a dash are missed, which leaves the line treated as before. Without a name set, nothing changes. The name is entered in Settings and kept only for the session; the demo script never addresses anyone by name, so this applies in live sessions. These are phrase matches, not judgments of meaning: paraphrased answers are missed, and some matches will be wrong. The browser preview has a separate simulation. Neither runs a language model or judges whether a cue was used. A `local-whisper` developer build already feeds local microphone transcription into these phrase rules; it does not provide semantic cue reasoning. The [synthetic transcription record](local-transcription.md) and the [first owner-observed hardware session](native-validation.md#live-session-check) have different evidence limits. System audio, native Stop/device acceptance and live cue usefulness remain unresolved.

A focus list (9 November 2026; first as a single goal line in Settings, then up to five lines the same day, moved into the notebook beside the session controls on 10 November because it changes with every meeting) lets a question through the pause that follows another cue, under any timing setting, and keeps it for 45 seconds, the length given to a question asked of the user by name (a topic change or correction still withdraws it, and a question being read is never replaced), when the line uses at least two of a focus line's identifying words (five letters or more, not common meeting words; three when the line has more than six). The Focused timing setting, also a switch on the companion, is the relevance filter the person sets for the meeting: only questions about a focus line, questions asked of them by name, and reminders of selected notes appear, so a conversation that drifts leaves the companion quiet rather than guessing. When a line sounds like the meeting ending ("before we finish", "to wrap up", "anything else", and a few more), the focus lines never touched are listed once as a Still open cue, for 45 seconds. A session shows at most three note reminders. The focus is the first thing besides the user's name that changes a lifecycle decision; every part of it is a word match on the person's own words, not a judgment about the meeting, and the companion is told only that a question is about the focus, apart from the Still open list, which names the untouched lines. In Focused mode the usual pause between questions applies; outside it a focus question skips the pause an unrelated cue left. The Still open list ignores the pause in every setting, waits for a clear companion, and a later wrap-up line retries until it has shown once. Fictional stress tests in `engine.rs` cover a meeting that stays on its focus, one that derails and wraps up, a question asked by name during Focused mode, and five selected notes. The focus list is also where a model first enters the product, deliberately at the edge: with Granola connected and its assistant available, Suggest from Granola (10 November 2026) asks that assistant one fixed question about the person's recent meetings and shows the answer as suggestions to tick. The model is Granola's, on Granola's side, before the meeting; its output never becomes a cue by itself; during the meeting everything stays rule-based and local.

A Said cue (10 November 2026) quotes a spoken date or commitment word for word: a sentence with a time word (a weekday, tomorrow, next week, a month and day, a count of days, including since 13 November 2026 "two working days" or "three full business days") and a commitment word (by, before, ready, starting, takes, due, send, and a few more), not a question and not hedged. It appears in place of a timing question the sentence just answered, or on its own after the fixed questions, for 18 seconds, and the same words are not quoted again within two minutes; in Focused mode only a line about the focus is quoted. Keep this line puts the quote, its time, and its speaker in the notes, an explicit action, after which the cue goes.

The product hypothesis behind the current cue set, in our own words: the valued moments in a meeting are a question put to the person, a spoken date or commitment, and a reminder from their own notes; prompting beyond those risks feeling like hand-holding. That is a claim for the pilot comparison to test, not a finding.

## The decision we want intelligence to help with

Use recent, source-linked context to decide whether assistance is still useful. Staying quiet is a valid result. Generate or display a cue only when it has an identifiable purpose and supported context; do not turn every ambiguity into an interruption.

- Suppress candidates already answered, duplicated, off-topic, superseded by transcript revisions, or too late to help.
- Re-evaluate after meaningful conversation changes instead of repeatedly sending the entire meeting on a fixed timer.
- Distinguish “someone asked a similar question” from “the issue is resolved.” An asked question may still be unanswered.
- Withdraw or mark resolved evidence when appropriate without replacing the text mid-read. Preserve manual dismissal and pause. A hold controls reading time, not whether a known-wrong claim remains valid.
- Abstain when sources, speaker attribution, or context are insufficient. Keep uncertainty visible in evaluation records.

Keep deterministic lifecycle and permission checks in Rust. A proposed semantic evaluator can return a bounded decision, a short cue if needed, and supporting source IDs. Validate that sources exist and the response matches the expected shape. The model must not control capture, export, file access, or external tools. Compare this design against simple rules before adding it to the live path.

## Learn from the conversation without a Save step

The ongoing conversation can provide possible outcome signals: a similar question was asked, an answer appeared naturally, a participant corrected the context, or the topic moved on. Use those signals first to manage the current session. With uncertain speaker identity, do not label a cue as used by the user.

Keep **observed use**, **resolution**, and **usefulness** distinct. A question can be repeated without helping; a reminder can help someone stay grounded without being spoken; a dismissed cue can have been useful already. Neither silence nor dismissal is an automatic negative training label. Similar speech does not prove that TalkQTE caused it.

Optional, low-effort feedback such as helpful, wrong context, or too late can supplement observed signals, but should not interrupt the call. Compare inferred outcomes against human labels in fictional scenarios before trusting them. Measure missed useful opportunities and incorrectly suppressed cues as well as displayed-cue precision.

Session adaptation, durable personal preferences, and training a shared model are separate decisions. Begin with bounded in-memory context. Persisting outcomes, uploading transcripts, or training on conversations is not implied by using a cue or connecting a note-taker. Follow [PRIVACY.md](../PRIVACY.md) and make any later retention/processing choice explicit. An unused cue should not become a meeting note or action item.

## Production remains a separate decision

Keep the semantic evaluator replaceable behind a narrow interface. A subscription experiment can test cue quality, but does not establish a dependable live service, acceptable tail latency, or frictionless onboarding. Do not require a coding subscription as the default distribution path without validating that choice with intended users. The local-first product plan remains the baseline; an optional hosted mode needs an explicit processing decision.

Anthropic's [product integration rules](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance) distinguish users signing into an unmodified Claude Code binary themselves from a developer routing everyone through a shared subscription or collecting their credentials. The documented binary integration has commercial terms and authentication conditions to review before distribution. Do not assume a personal lab setup grants a general-purpose hosted inference entitlement. Recheck Cursor's product integration terms before choosing it for distribution as well.

Measure conversational performance directly. Coding benchmarks and model size do not establish whether a model can judge when to stay quiet, and Opus is not automatically the right choice for a time-sensitive cue. A smaller model may earn the live role if it meets the same quality criteria sooner and uses less allowance.

## Rules baseline on the shared suite

The current phrase rules were run on the fictional cases the [Claude lab](claude-cue-lab.md) uses, through two offline replays using the live-session engine, with no model. Events are spaced two seconds apart because the fixtures carry no times.

- **Replay** (`rules_baseline_on_the_shared_fictional_suite` in `engine.rs`): the rules must raise every cue themselves. "No cue raised" means the case starts with a visible cue, but the rules never raise one from the first line, so they cannot take part.
- **From the fixture's cue** (`rules_from_each_fixtures_own_starting_cue`, added 13 November 2026): where a case lists a visible cue, the test puts that cue in place after its source line, as the lab does, and the rules only decide what happens next. The replay matched 5 of 11 expected actions on 9 November 2026 (3 of the original eight) and matches 7 of 11 on 13 November 2026 (5 of the original eight). Two rule changes account for the difference:

- `topic-moved-on`: "Let's leave the cover choice for another meeting." now counts as a topic change and withdraws the unread decision question.
- `context-reminder`: "Legal needs two full working days after the draft is ready." is now quoted as a Said cue, because a count such as "two full working days" is recognized as a time. The action matches the label, but the cue does not: the label describes a reminder raised at "Can we commit to Friday?", and the rules only quote the earlier line when it is said. They do not connect the two lines. Counting actions only, as the lab comparison does, this is a match; if it is not counted, the replay matches 6 of 11.

The 9 of 11 in the last column is not a rule improvement over 7 of 11. It measures a different thing: what the rules do once the fixture's cue is on screen. Two cases the rules cannot enter by replay (`visible-cue-resolved`, `uncertain-speaker`) match from that starting state; "I'll take care of sending the checklist" resolves the owner question, and "Who's sending that?" changes nothing. The two rule changes above account for two of the nine; this measurement was not run on the earlier rules.

Cases that still differ, and why they were left alone:

- `already-answered` (expected quiet; rules raise the owner question, then withdraw it when "I'll send it" arrives). No phrase can fix this: the answer is in a later line. Whether the user ever sees the question depends on how far apart the lines arrive, which the fixture does not say. Waiting a moment before showing any question would turn this into quiet at the cost of showing every question later; that is a timing decision against the [trigger-to-cue budget](budgets.md), not a rule change.
- `context-corrected` (expected withdraw; rules stay quiet). "Actually, the supplier review is staying where it is" has no explicit correction phrase. Knowing that "staying where it is" takes back "is moving soon" needs judgment of meaning; treating every "Actually, …" as a correction would withdraw questions when someone only adds a detail.
- `visible-cue-resolved` and `uncertain-speaker` by replay (no cue raised). The first line is "We should send the revised checklist." Raising an owner question for every "we should…" would guess that no owner is implied and would interrupt far more often.
- `context-reminder`'s reminder itself, as described above: connecting "two full working days" to "Friday" on a Tuesday is reasoning the rules do not do.

The [Claude lab record](claude-cue-lab.md#validation-record) covers three runs per case on 8–10 November: 10 of 11 expected actions matched in every pass (7 of eight), with no action changing between runs. No lab run was made for the 13 November changes. The sole lab disagreement is `missing-owner`, where staying quiet to avoid duplicating the speakers' own question is defensible; the expected label still needs human review and is unchanged here, so the rules' "show" counts as a match only while that label stands. All 33 request times span 2,969–5,768 ms including CLI startup. The 22 repeat requests averaged about 155 reported output tokens each; this is not a billing or allowance measure.

These counts describe the two prototypes on eleven fictional cases, not an accuracy estimate for equivalent evaluators or real meetings. The 13 November rules were written with these cases in view, so the suite no longer tests them independently; a larger suite with cases written by someone else is needed to estimate how often the new phrases withdraw a question wrongly. The [held-out cases](#held-out-cases) below are a first step.

The owner rule recognizes "someone can", "someone should", and "someone could". Volunteer verbs include "send", "sort", and "book", allowing `paraphrased-use` to resolve. The suite's questions beginning "Who's" are outside the open-question rule. The `context-reminder` case supplies no imported note, so it does not exercise the app's title-word reminder feature. Whatever the trigger, the owner question's wording is fixed ("Who will take the first draft?"), so on these fixtures it asks about a draft while the speakers discuss a checklist. Paraphrased triggers, corrections without a correction phrase, and topic shifts in other words remain gaps.

Three judgments per case on fictional text establish only this limited repeatability. A larger suite, reviewed labels, and the planned comparison of useful participation and listening comprehension are still needed before choosing a live evaluator.

### Outcome labels

Three labeled cases were added on 9 November 2026 to cover outcome signals the first eight lacked. Each now has three authorized lab runs in the [validation record](claude-cue-lab.md#validation-record); their actions matched the expected labels in every run. Further runs require new authorization:

- `context-corrected`: a speaker withdraws the premise behind a visible cue that asks for a new date ("Actually, the supplier review is staying where it is"). Expected: withdraw. The correction removes the cue's basis without answering it.
- `paraphrased-use`: the participant asks the visible cue's question in other words, and another speaker answers. Expected: resolved, with observed use "possible". Use and resolution are labeled separately; similar speech is a possible signal of use, not proof that the cue caused it or helped.
- `repeated-trigger`: a speaker restates the line that raised a visible cue. Expected: quiet, because a second cue would duplicate the one already shown. The rules raise the first cue and leave it in place without raising another after the repeated line two seconds later.

Across the suite, labels keep observed use and resolution apart; no case labels usefulness or involves dismissal. These are labels written by the developer, not outcomes observed in meetings.
