# Run the prototype

What the current prototype does once it is running, with its limits. Setup is in the [README](../README.md#run-the-current-prototype); the developer-only live session is in [Live session](../README.md#live-session-developer-build).

The notebook opens with its editor and cue area. Expand **Set up a live session** in the sidebar to find the **Before your first meeting** checklist: your name, optional focus lines, the speech model when the build can use one, and prior notes; it steps aside once a name and any needed model are ready, or with **Not now** for the session, and returns on the next launch because nothing is kept between launches. The moments TalkQTE acts on are small and concrete: someone asks you something by name, someone says a date or commits to one, or the conversation touches a note you brought. Choose **Try demo** for the fictional community-garden conversation, spoken by two synthetic voices bundled with the app (**Mute voices** for text only; see the [voiced demo record](voiced-demo.md)). It requires no capture permission, model download, or API credentials. Open **Why?** to inspect a question's source and keep it available while reading; **Dismiss** clears it. Untouched questions pass quietly. When someone says a date or commits to one ("ready by Friday", "legal takes two days"), the companion quotes that sentence word for word as a **Said** cue instead of paraphrasing it, because "ready by Friday" is not "starting Friday"; **Keep this line** puts the quote, with its time and speaker, into your notes, and nothing is kept unless you press it. New questions expand briefly, with a reduced-motion alternative. Settings provides text size, timing, companion placement, and a session-only name used to notice questions asked of you in a live session. Expand the notebook's **This meeting** section below the editor and cue area to reach the focus list, up to five short lines on what matters in this meeting, with **Clear** to start the next meeting fresh: when a line uses a focus line's words, a question about it comes through even during the pause after another question and stays 45 seconds, the length of a question asked of you by name; a topic change still withdraws it. The **Focused** timing setting, also a switch on the companion's bar, shows only those questions, questions asked of you by name, and reminders of notes you chose, so a meeting that drifts leaves the companion quiet; when someone says the meeting is wrapping up, the focus lines the conversation never touched are listed once. A session shows at most three note reminders. All of these are word matches, kept only until you quit.

The companion is configured not to take keyboard focus; use the notebook for keyboard controls. Drag it by its top bar to put it anywhere on the display; choosing a position in Settings moves it back. Dismissal there returns focus to notes or the selected Transcript tab unless you have already moved elsewhere. Saving an export returns focus to **Export**, **Try demo** or **Replay demo** moves it to **Pause** when the pressed button disappears, and **Question appearance & timing** opens Settings with focus on the first appearance control; browser checks cover these, not the native app. Cross-app focus and native placement still have [pending checks](native-validation.md). See [Glance view](glance-mode.md) for the current hold and expiry behavior.

**Prior context from a file:** open **Prior context** in the notebook; **Add notes from a file** reads a file you choose, up to 1 MB: one Markdown (`.md`) or plain-text (`.txt`) note, such as a summary exported from another note-taker, or a TalkQTE JSON notes file (a `notes` list with an `id`, `date`, and `text` for each note; up to 200 notes are read). Up to 20 notes can be kept. A Markdown or plain-text note takes its title from a front matter `title:` or a `# ` heading on its first line (Markdown only), or else the file name, and its date only from a `date:` in front matter or a `Date: YYYY-MM-DD` line near the top, before any later heading; otherwise the chooser asks you for the date, and TalkQTE never guesses one. Its text is kept as written, without Markdown formatting ([how files are read](note-file-formats.md)). You tick the notes to keep, and they are listed in that section labeled as not from this conversation; while the section is closed, its row shows only the note count and whether Granola is connected. They stay in memory for the session, can be removed one by one or all at once, and are never exported. When a line of the conversation uses two or more distinctive words from a note's title, the companion shows that note once as a reminder, labeled with its date and "not from this conversation"; this is a phrase match on the title, not a judgment of relevance, and whether such reminders help is an untested hypothesis. **Prior context from Granola:** in the desktop app, **Connect Granola** in the same section signs you in to your own Granola account in your browser (OAuth, with a return to the app on this Mac) and keeps the sign-in in memory until you quit or disconnect; **Choose Granola notes** then lists your notes of the last 30 days, the window Granola's free plan allows, and only the notes you tick are brought in, through the same chooser, with at most ten selected per Granola request. TalkQTE asks Granola for its note list, the ticked notes, and, if you ask for suggestions, one fixed question; it reads no transcripts (a paid Granola feature) and writes nothing back. The connection follows Granola's documented MCP server ([integration plan](product-readiness.md#integration-readiness)); sign-in and focus suggestions have worked in an account check. The chooser reads the tagged listing structure from that check, including the title, ID, and ISO meeting date, and ignores participant and folder content. It shows at most 200 notes from the returned page and reports any additional notes the provider leaves out; automatic pagination is not implemented. Listing now works in the owner’s account check. Selected-note import reads the observed `private_notes` and `summary` fields, keeping their labels and excluding participant, folder, attachment, and provider-notice text. Empty or unavailable private notes do not hide a summary. Excerpts keep at most 2,000 characters, so long notes are not imported in full. Reimporting replaces a kept note only when Granola reports a newer update timestamp; an unchanged or older response preserves the copy. Notes without a valid update timestamp can be imported, but must be removed first to refresh their copy. Reimporting is the only way to refresh a copy: a single **Check for updates** step exists in the code, but a signed-in Granola connection does not offer it, because Granola reports no workspace identifier for it to verify ([scope](selected-note-refresh-plan.md#first-slice-one-explicit-check-simulated-provider-only)). If Granola omits a selected note, the request fails without importing a partial selection; retry with fewer notes. The chooser limits each Granola import to 10 notes, matching the provider’s request limit; the session still holds at most 20. Errors appear inside the chooser, selected checkboxes remain checked for retry, and Cancel discards a pending import. The optional developer trace prints a bounded JSON structure and short tag outline, masking arbitrary keys and all scalar content, including tool descriptions and schema defaults. Disconnect discards late listing/import/suggestion results even after reconnecting; a request already running may finish, and imported copies remain until removed. Removing a note also clears a reminder quoting it, even while held open. Headless Rust tests now run the tagged response through a local fictional MCP server, the import attempt, and the context store; Chromium/WebKit tests cover the chooser controls. These fictional checks alone do not establish a real-note import. Separately, on 11 November the owner reported that adding and removing a Granola note succeeded in the native app; refresh, failure recovery and detailed data-flow acceptance remain. When the connected account offers Granola's assistant (the `query_granola_meetings` tool, which depends on the plan), the **This meeting** box adds **Suggest from Granola** under the focus list: TalkQTE asks the assistant one fixed question about your meetings of the last 30 days (open decisions, tasks nobody has taken on, unanswered questions; short lines, no names) and shows the answer as suggestions you tick before any becomes a focus line. This is the only place TalkQTE uses a language model today (the speech model runs locally), it is Granola's model on Granola's side, it runs only when you press the button, and a suggestion is Granola's reading of your notes, not a fact.

**Demo notes are kept in memory until you quit.** Review and export anything you want to retain. Optional notes and export support prototype exploration; saving generated cues is not part of the live flow.

<details>
<summary>Microphone activity and development commands</summary>

Choose **Start microphone** explicitly and grant macOS permission if prompted. The permission text says the microphone is used only after you start it, to show whether there is input and, during a live session, to transcribe speech on this Mac; the prompt has not yet been observed with this wording. **Stop microphone** releases the input device; the stopping state remains until release completes. Pausing or ending the fictional demo does not stop monitoring. Hiding the companion only hides its window. The monitor does not retain samples, upload audio, hear the other side of a call through system audio, or generate cues. The browser preview cannot monitor a microphone. On one Mac, the permission prompt, signal activity, and Stop releasing the microphone have been checked ([record](native-validation.md#microphone-activity-check)); permission denial, device disconnect, and other setups remain unchecked.

`npm run dev` serves a separate browser simulation at `http://127.0.0.1:1420`. `npm test` runs browser behavior checks; `npm run build` builds the frontend. Browser checks do not verify Rust IPC or native windows.

`APPLE_SIGNING_IDENTITY=- npm run tauri -- build --debug --bundles app` builds an ad-hoc-signed local development app. It is not a Developer ID-signed or notarized release. The bundle requires macOS 14.0 or later and carries the generated third-party notices at `Contents/Resources/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt`; the app does not display them. `npm run check:release` checks the release configuration offline and signs nothing; the [release path](release-path.md) lists the steps that still need the owner's Apple Developer account.
