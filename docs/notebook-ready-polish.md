# Notebook states and limits

The Notebook separates ready, live, ended, and error-recovery states while preserving Direction C tokens, uniform all-round borders, and every existing capture, privacy, and Stop control:

- **Ready:** emphasizes **Start live session** paired with its compact contextual disclosure (sources, providers, and until Stop). Working notes remain editable on the left; **Export Markdown…** stays hidden while nothing exists to export and appears in the notes footer once you write notes (opening the selectable Markdown export review dialog). A compact **Cues** standby card sits beside notes directly above progressive disclosures for **This meeting** (focus) and **Prior context**.
- **Live:** keeps **Stop live session** (or **Cancel start**), **Microphone**, and **Cue processing** (including per-session consent, pause, turn-off, and independent transcript retention disclosure) compact and reachable above the fold, giving the active **CueCard** and **Notes / Transcript / Ask** primary space. **Transcript** and **Ask** cross-link directly (`Ask about transcript`, `View transcript`, cited-line highlight, and `Back to Ask`), and **Ask** states the current bounded recent excerpt window (capped at 200 lines / 64 KiB) alongside its full scope/cost disclosure.
- **Ended & error recovery:** groups **Meeting notes** and **Saved to Meetings / Keep this meeting** into one post-session outcomes band with outcome-named **Export Markdown…** in the ended session actions and saved-meeting detail (**Meetings**). Transcription key/auth failures include an inline **Open Settings** recovery action.
- **Build provenance & Settings:** the sidebar footer mounts **BuildVersion**, exposing native source commit and dirty/profile/target details on desktop and `Browser preview` in non-native previews. Persistent controls live in **Settings**, organized into five scannable groups (**Providers & keys**, **Cues & companion**, **Capture & prompts**, **Meetings & storage**, **Privacy & setup**) with top section jump links.

The idle cue explanation names the configured cue provider when live status supplies one. It describes a possible source-backed cue, not guaranteed useful output. An unavailable build or configuration keeps its reason visible and points to Settings. Phrase-based rules still exist alongside model cues; a ready own-key session is not described as a phrase-rules-only build.

Starting still immediately enables the capture and provider sending disclosed beside Start. The active microphone/source controls, Cancel start, Stop, cue-processing review, and recovery errors retain their behavior. Export stays hidden when nothing exists to export and while a live session is running. No new context retrieval, provider request, approval, or data-sharing scope is introduced. Optional navigation callbacks let prior-context availability guidance open the existing Meetings or Settings view.

Working notes are an editable in-memory copy, not a continuously saved document. By default, stopping a non-empty live meeting saves it locally; Settings can change that default. Already kept meetings remain until deleted, while exports and backups have separate lifecycles. Editing the working copy after Stop does not mean an existing kept meeting has been updated: use its explicit save/update control. Storage remains unencrypted. The Notebook footer describes retention without implying that transcription and cue processing stay on-device. See [meeting history](meeting-history.md), [meeting notes](meeting-notes.md), and [privacy](../PRIVACY.md).

## Acceptance and remaining work

Fictional browser checks cover ready, unavailable, preparing, running, paused, stopping, failure/retry, and ended states; keyboard controls, editable notes/export review, Transcript/Ask cross-navigation, grouped Settings, and narrow layouts remain part of that journey. [Direction C specimens](brand/notebook-ready.html) show these states using shared tokens and app styles. They are static illustrations, not a running capture implementation.

Browser stand-ins do not establish native capture, key-store access, webview accessibility, successful real provider calls, or live cue usefulness. The next readiness blocker is the complete native start → useful cue → Stop/recovery → saved-meeting journey. Acceptance requires synthetic native calls on supported platforms with provider disclosures, focus, release of capture, and saved content checked explicitly; live cue usefulness needs a separate, consented evaluation.
