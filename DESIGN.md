# TalkQTE interface

The interface is a quiet meeting companion with a friendly notebook. Rounded forms, readable type, and generous actions make a question approachable without adding pressure to answer. This is an implementation direction, not a final brand identity.

- Deep evergreen navigation, warm paper work areas, dark ink, and muted terracotta for the primary demo action.
- Rounded system type where the platform supplies it, with native sans-serif fallbacks. Body uses 16px text. A cue is 22px with its source at 14px, or 28px and 16px at the larger setting; in the 360px companion the cue steps down to 20px, or 25px with a 15px source. A cue longer than 90 characters is set a step smaller, and one longer than 140 two steps, so the whole of it stays on the card. Tabular numerals are reserved for elapsed time and counts.
- One sidebar holds Notebook navigation, optional first-run setup and Settings beside a spacious note editor. There is no meeting list or search: nothing is kept between launches, so there is only ever the current session. Optional first-run setup and meeting focus expand on request; notebook tabs use an underline and the editor sits on the paper surface. Use spacing and rules instead of decorative cards. Capture status, errors and Stop remain visible. The active question appears in a dedicated companion area or a separate desktop window.
- Show one complete cue that is understood at a glance, with nothing to click. Its first words say what it is ("You could ask", "Just said", "Asked you", "From your notes"), and the line or imported note it came from is quoted under it. A thin line between the two shows how long an untouched cue has left: it has no numbers, it stops while the demo is paused, and it is the Hold control. Hold keeps the cue until it is dismissed, and the line then fills and rests; the word Hold shows when the pointer or keyboard is on the card. An "Asked you" question has no line, only "Stays longer · no countdown". Dismiss is a corner cross. Cues support the current conversation; there is no Save action or saved-question workflow. Held cues remain actionable while paused and after playback finishes; they say when they are from earlier, and a later answer is quoted above their source. Untouched cues expire quietly. There is no countdown in numbers, scrolling script, or response score. See [Glance view evidence](docs/glance-mode.md).
- The desktop companion defaults to the bottom-right of the notebook display, with optional top-center, top-left, and top-right presets in Settings. Its resting bar is 320 × 76 logical pixels. The bar is one quiet line: what is being captured as a glyph with a dot while it is on (a microphone, or application audio in development builds), then its controls as icons with names and tooltips. Stop is always there while anything is captured; Pause and Hide sit beside it; there is no title and no focus switch, which is in Settings. The glyphs are muted at rest and never below 3:1 against the bar. A state the glyph cannot say (starting, stopping, an error, cues paused, the scripted demo) is spelled out in words, and nothing suggests capture when none is happening. A question uses 340 of height whether or not it is held, and a width that follows the work area: 360 on one narrower than 1100 logical pixels, 480 on one wider than 2400, 420 between. Larger text adds 80 pixels of height. All sizes are clamped to the work area, with scrolling when needed. Bottom placement keeps its bottom-right corner; top presets keep their top edge. A paused question stays visible. The bar returns on dismissal or untouched expiry.
- Use 16px control corners and 24–28px panel/question corners, with consistent stroke icons. The native window remains opaque. On macOS, AppKit supplies the rounded outer frame around full-size content; the title and window buttons are hidden using public APIs. Other platforms retain a borderless window.
- Primary actions are at least 48px high and icon controls at least 44px in each dimension. Keep a clear focus ring on both paper and forest surfaces. Separators organize content without enclosing every paragraph in a card.
- Hover, focus, loading, error, empty, paused, and ended states are part of the interface. A new cue comes in from the left over 200ms and opens sideways to its full width over 520ms. Everything is in place from the first frame; the purpose and the source line are at full strength at once. A suggested cue's words darken in reading order within three quarters of a second. A Said quote and a later answer fill from dim to full in order at about the pace they were said, about two words part-filled at a time, done within about six and a half seconds however long the line; this is a replay at an even pace, not the speaker's own timing. Waiting words are never lighter than 55% strength for the large bold cue (3.2:1) or 68% for smaller text (4.6:1). The time line draws in behind them. Timer updates do not repeat the arrival. Holding fills the line over 560ms. A later answer to a held cue opens in place over 380ms with a small tick. A dismissed or expired cue folds away over 190ms and takes no input while it goes. On macOS the companion window keeps the cue's size for 220ms so the fold shows, then returns to its bar; with Reduce Motion on, and on other platforms, it returns at once. Reduced motion removes these animations and control transitions while preserving all content and actions, and the time line then moves in four steps. No bounce, pulsing, or celebratory animation competes with the meeting. Native window resizing is immediate apart from that wait; the animation belongs to the rendered question.
- All sample content is labeled demo. No fake capture status, model readiness, integrations, or persistence guarantees.
