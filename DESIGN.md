# TalkQTE interface

The interface is a quiet meeting companion with a calm notebook, following brand Direction C (White and studio amber; defined in `docs/brand/BRAND_GUIDE.md` and applied in `src/tokens.css`). Crisp typography, restrained contrast, and generous targets keep cues readable at a glance without adding pressure to answer.

- **Palette (Direction C: White and studio amber).** White work areas (`#ffffff`), drafting vellum sidebar (`#f3f5f7`), quiet off-white notebook cue cards (`#f8f7f4`), deep carbon ink (`#11151b`), slate muted ink (`#4f5a68`), neutral hairline dividers (`#d3d9e2`), and calibrated control borders (`#737f90`). Primary actions (`Start live session` and confirmation buttons) use a carbon fill (`#11151b`, hover `#252c36`) with white text (`#ffffff`). Amber ink (`#875500`) is used on light surfaces for text accents, step markers, links, the active cue time fill, the `Asked you` border and label, and the keyboard focus ring. Studio solar amber (`#ebb45b`) is reserved for dark companion surfaces, fills paired with carbon ink, and the app mark; it is never used as text on light surfaces (1.87:1). The floating companion bar and shell use void carbon (`#0d1014`) with signal white (`#f5f7fa`), telemetry grey (`#9aa4b2`), quiet status (`#7d8a9b`), and solar amber (`#ebb45b`) for live status and focus.
- **Typography and scale.** Self-hosted `Manrope Variable` (SIL OFL 1.1, `public/fonts/manrope-latin-wght-normal.woff2`) is used for body, headings, controls, and the cue itself; self-hosted `IBM Plex Mono` (`public/fonts/ibm-plex-mono-latin-{400,500}-normal.woff2`) is used for small labels, step markers, status tags, and elapsed time. No network fonts are loaded; the earlier Georgia serif heading experiment is retired. One spacing scale (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 px) structures the window. Body uses 16px text, secondary lines use 14px in muted ink, and paragraphs keep a readable measure (at most 68 characters). In the notebook a cue is 22px with its source at 14px, or 28px and 16px at the larger setting. The companion is a small prompt beside a call, not a second window: its cue is 17px, close to a meeting's own captions, with a 13px source, or 21px and 15px at the larger setting. A cue longer than 90 characters is set a step smaller, and one longer than 140 two steps, so the whole of it stays on the card. Tabular numerals are reserved for elapsed time and counts.
- **Mark.** `public/mark.svg` uses the Direction C Calibrated Cue mark (a void carbon rounded tile with subtle frame, crisp framing corners, and a solar amber bar).
- **Layout and rules.** One vellum sidebar holds Notebook navigation, optional first-run setup, and Settings beside a spacious white note editor; its surface runs the full height of the window as the workspace scrolls. There is no meeting list or search: nothing is kept between launches, so there is only ever the current session. Optional first-run setup and meeting focus expand on request; active notebook tabs use a `text-decoration` underline (never a one-sided border) and the editor sits on the white paper surface. Use spacing and neutral 1px hairline rules instead of decorative cards: the idle microphone check sits as one quiet row, and Cue processing is separated by a top hairline rule rather than an enclosing card, with uppercase mono `Step 1 of 2` and `Step 2 of 2` markers above its two approval headings, mono keys and hairline rows in its review table, and a muted `Development build` heading above a dashed hairline when development or lab controls apply. Capture status, errors (uniform 1px border all round) and Stop remain visible. The active question appears in a dedicated companion area or a separate desktop window.
- **No one-sided accent borders.** Cards, cues, notes, callouts, alerts, quotes, chips, and list items never use an accent border on one side only (no left, right, top, or bottom accent borders, inset `box-shadow` strips, pseudo-element bars, or positioned strip elements). Only full-width neutral 1px hairlines used as section or row dividers are allowed and explicitly listed in `scripts/check-one-sided-borders.mjs`, which runs in CI (`npm run check:borders`).
- **Glance cues and "Asked you".** Show one complete cue that is understood at a glance, with nothing to click. Its uppercase mono label row (`6px` square marker in amber ink) says what it is ("You could ask", "Just said", "Asked you", "From your notes"), above the cue statement, with the line or imported note it came from quoted under it. A thin line between the two shows how long an untouched cue has left: it has no numbers, and it is the Hold control. Hold keeps the cue until it is dismissed, and the line then fills and rests; the word Hold shows when the pointer or keyboard is on the card. An "Asked you" question has no line, only "Stays longer · no countdown". Being asked by name is the one cue that expects an answer from the person, so it is told apart before any reading, without a one-sided bar and not by colour alone: a quiet warm tint across the whole card (`#fdf6e9`), a full 1.5px border all round in amber ink (`#875500`, kept in `CanvasText` under forced colours), an `@` mark before its label, and the label itself in amber ink, in the notebook and the companion alike. In the companion its "Stays longer · no countdown" words give way to a plain rule; Hold still carries them for assistive technology. Every other kind (a suggested or fixed question, an open question to the group, a focus reminder, a Said quote, a note reminder) shares the calm default (`1px` calibrated border on `#f8f7f4` in the notebook; `1px` neutral hairline on pure white `#ffffff` in the dark companion dock), so the distinction stays meaningful; their first words already say what they are. Dismiss is a corner cross. Cues support the current conversation; there is no Save action or saved-question workflow. A held cue stays actionable until it is dismissed or the session ends; they say when they are from earlier, and a later answer is quoted above their source. Untouched cues expire quietly. There is no countdown in numbers, scrolling script, or response score. See [Glance view evidence](docs/glance-mode.md).
- **Desktop companion.** The desktop companion defaults to the bottom-right of the notebook display, with optional top-center, top-left, and top-right presets in Settings. Its resting bar is 320 × 76 logical pixels. The bar is one quiet line: what is being captured as a glyph with a dot while it is on (a microphone, or application audio in development builds), then its controls as icons with names and tooltips. Stop is always there while anything is captured; Pause and Hide sit beside it; there is no title and no focus switch, which is in Settings. The glyphs are muted at rest and never below 3:1 against the bar. A state the glyph cannot say (starting, stopping, an error, cues paused) is spelled out in words, and nothing suggests capture when none is happening. A question uses 232 of height whether or not it is held, and keeps the bar's 320 width on every display; larger text adds 44. Under the cue the companion shows only the line it cites, at most two lines of it, with no second quote; the notebook shows every source. With the bottom-right placement the window keeps its bottom edge, so the bar sits at the bottom and the card is above it; the top presets, and a window the person has dragged, keep their top edge and the bar is at the top. The bar stays first for the keyboard either way. All sizes are clamped to the work area, with scrolling when needed. Bottom placement keeps its bottom-right corner; top presets keep their top edge. A paused question stays visible. The bar returns on dismissal or untouched expiry.
- **Corners and window framing.** Use 2px control corners, 4px popover corners, 8px panel and notebook cue corners, 10px export dialog corners, and 18px outer dock shell / 13px dock cue card corners, with consistent stroke icons. The native window remains opaque. On macOS, AppKit supplies the rounded outer frame around full-size content; the title and window buttons are hidden using public APIs. Other platforms retain a borderless window.
- **Targets and focus.** Primary actions are at least 48px high and icon controls at least 44px in each dimension. Keep a clear focus ring on both light surfaces (`#875500`) and dark companion surfaces (`#ebb45b`). Separators organize content without enclosing every paragraph in a card.
- **States and motion.** Hover, focus, loading, error, empty, paused, and ended states are part of the interface. In the notebook a new cue comes in from the left over 200ms and opens sideways to its full width over 480ms. In the companion it grows out of the bar over 300ms, quick at the start: down from a bar at the top, uncovering the cue first, or up from a bar at the bottom with its words riding up with its top edge like a drawer, so either way the cue is in view from the first frame; the cue settles first and its source follows 90ms later, moving only, and no words overlap at any moment. A cue does not fade in, and every word of the cue is uncovered within 200ms. Everything is in place from the first frame; the purpose and the source line are at full strength at once. A suggested cue's words darken in reading order within three quarters of a second. A Said quote and a later answer fill from dim to full in order at about the pace they were said, about two words part-filled at a time, done within about six and a half seconds however long the line; this is a replay at an even pace, not the speaker's own timing. Waiting words are never lighter than 55% strength for the large bold cue (3.97:1 on `#f8f7f4`) or 68% for smaller text (6.17:1 on `#f8f7f4`, 6.13:1 on `#fdf6e9`). The time line draws in behind them. Timer updates do not repeat the arrival. Holding fills the line over 560ms. A later answer to a held cue opens in place over 380ms with a small tick. A cue that goes, whether dismissed, expired, withdrawn or answered, folds away over 190ms (in the companion, back into the bar) and takes no input while it goes. A new cue never waits for it: the old card goes at once and the new one grows in its place. In the companion, stopping the session removes the card at once, with no fold. On macOS the companion window keeps the cue's size for 220ms so the fold shows, then returns to its bar; with Reduce Motion on, and on other platforms, it returns at once. Reduced motion removes these animations and control transitions while preserving all content and actions, and the time line then moves in four steps. No bounce, pulsing, or celebratory animation competes with the meeting. Native window resizing is immediate apart from that wait; the animation belongs to the rendered question.
- **Session honesty.** The notebook is about the person's own session and has a state of its own before a session, during one and after Stop. Before a session it says that nothing is being captured and what a live session needs; afterwards it says that nothing was saved. It carries no sample content: fictional material appears only in tests and lab recordings, where it is labeled. No fake capture status, model readiness, integrations, or persistence guarantees.
